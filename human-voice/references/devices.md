# Devices: hooks, turns, closers, swaps

Learn the mechanism, then write your own line. A device you reuse every time becomes a tic.

## Hooks (the first line)

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| A specific number with tension | "Our weekly meeting costs us 11 hours of senior time. It produces two decisions." |
| A scene, in present tense | "It's 4:55 on a Friday and the deploy button is glowing." |
| The answer, flat | "Yes, you can freeze bread. You should." |
| The reader's own thought | "You've read this kind of email before, and you skimmed it." |
| A true, odd detail | "The fridge makes bread go stale faster than the counter does." |
| A gentle correction | "Close, but the problem isn't the database." |

Avoid: questions you then answer yourself ("Ever wondered why…?"), dictionary definitions, "In today's world",
and announcing the topic ("In this post, I'll explore…").

## Turns (the tension)

- "That said, …" / "The catch: …" / "Here's what that costs."
- "This works until …"
- "I got this wrong for two years."
- "The strongest objection is …, and it's half right."
- Size the other side honestly: "a loud minority", "one person, but they had the data".

## Closers (the last line)

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| The next step | "Cancel one recurring meeting this week and see who notices." |
| A callback to the hook | "Somewhere, a deploy button is still glowing. Let it." |
| Deadpan understatement | "Probably fine." / "Ten out of ten, no notes." |
| A real quote that sums it up | As the on-call engineer put it: "Boring is the goal." |
| The plain verdict, restated | "So: freeze it, don't fridge it." |

A closer is one line. If it needs explaining, cut it.

## Swap table: slop to plain

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| leverage, utilise | use |
| delve into, dive into | look at, explain (or just start) |
| a testament to | shows |
| navigate the landscape of | deal with, handle |
| robust, seamless, cutting-edge | say what it actually does ("never dropped a request in 6 months") |
| game-changer, revolutionary | the number that changed |
| It's important to note that | (delete, then say it) |
| I hope this email finds you well | (delete) |
| I wanted to reach out to | (delete, then ask) |
| Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally | (delete; or "Also", "And") |
| In conclusion / Overall | (delete the paragraph; end on the last useful line) |
| It's not just X, it's Y | Y. (say the real claim) |
| empower, unlock, elevate | the verb for what actually happens ("lets you", "cuts", "adds") |
| We take this very seriously | what you are doing about it, with a date |
| excited to announce | what you shipped and who it helps |
| serves as, stands as, boasts, features | is, has |
| …, highlighting / underscoring / ensuring X | (cut the tail; if X matters, give it its own sentence) |
| plays a pivotal / crucial role | what it actually does, with a number |
| reflects broader trends, setting the stage | (delete; or name the trend and the evidence) |
| vibrant, renowned, nestled in the heart of | the one detail that shows it |
| experts argue, industry reports say | the named person or report, or "I think" |
| challenges and future prospects | (delete the section; put the real risk in the turn) |
| the tool… the platform… the solution | one name, every time |
| **Label:** text, on every bullet | plain sentences, unless the reader scans by label |

## Rhythm check

Read the draft aloud. If three sentences in a row have the same length and shape, break one: make it very short,
or join two. Lists of three that exist only for rhythm get cut to the two that matter.

## Sentence moves that carry a voice

| Move | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| The button: a short sentence after a long one | "It still paged someone about once a fortnight. Loyal to the end." | The short line lands like a beat, and the voice lives in it |
| The aside in brackets | "Six claims (two of them about the coffee)" | It sounds like you leaning in, said once and quietly |
| The reframe | "That's not a usage limit; that's a restraining order." | It's a "not X, it's Y" used as a joke, not as an argument |
| The spoken opener | "Okay, so." / "Look." / "Short version:" | It sets a loose register before the tight facts |
| The understated number | "The estimate said two weeks. Close." | The gap between the number and the comment is the joke |
| The coined label | "the 'I have one of those!' effect" | It takes the room's own words, so it sums up and jokes at once |

Use at most one or two of these per short piece. Every one of them turns into a tic if repeated.
